# Results draft for the corrected-outcome candidate views

These alternative views use `ebb_v102_20261019_677ed97`, `s30b_v11_20261019_5482c43` and `fsb_v5_20261019_8212df4`. The preceding text is preserved in `history/ov1_results_text.md`. The original analysis definitions were rerun after earlier results had been inspected; the refreshed views are not independent replication.

Early home-cage movement predicted later Integrated Resilience within this cohort. The movement-only model attained leave-one-animal-out R² = 0.218. Batch means were retained in both the predictor and outcome. The permutation comparison and repeated-split estimates are shown in the candidate prediction panels; the repeated-split percentile range describes variation across splits and is not a confidence interval for the leave-one-animal-out estimate. This internal predictive association does not establish transport to a new cohort or a causal behavioural mechanism.

The exploratory light-phase sustained-positional-inactivity contrasts no longer meet the local BH decision threshold of 0.05 after correction of the later outcome. The RES minus SUS estimate was 0.0028 in females (95% CI 0.0001 to 0.0056; local q = 0.14) and −0.0022 in males (95% CI −0.0048 to 0.0005; local q = 0.18). The point estimates and intervals are displayed as exploratory estimates. Opposite signs alone do not establish a difference between sexes. The movement-adjusted female estimate was 0.0008 (95% CI −0.000000 to 0.0015); its interval includes zero. The measured quantity is sustained positional inactivity, rather than EEG-defined sleep.

The home-cage cookie views describe the change in position-change rate following placement and its association with the later outcome. Outcome-association slopes were imprecise: females, −0.00273 (95% CI −0.0175 to 0.0121); males, 0.00142 (95% CI −0.0163 to 0.0191). The raw interaction P was 0.65. Both slope intervals include zero, and these results do not establish a reproducible relationship with later Integrated Resilience. These activity measurements do not resolve motivation or eating behaviour.

The domain heatmaps summarize stored group contrasts. Their robustness strips distinguish changes at the finer time scale, batch and cage dependence, low sample size and an unassessable check. A marker in the primary heatmap is not by itself evidence that its contrast is robust across these checks. The HMM view displays state occupancy and bout summaries with the stored reporting gates; states are pooled and do not support sex-level state effects.
