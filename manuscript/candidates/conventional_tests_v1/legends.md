Candidates only; the numbering follows the free publication identities (`extended_data_10`, `extended_data_11`) and may change at submission. Source: SISanalyzer bundle `ctp_v1_20261016_e276430` (`source_data/SISanalyzer/`).

**Extended Data Fig. 10 | Conventional behaviour tests and locomotion, females and males analysed separately.**
**a**, Main readout of each test: elevated plus maze (share of time in the open arms), open field (time in the centre), novel object recognition (novel-object preference D2), social preference (social preference D2 and social novelty preference D2). Dots, individual animals (CON slate blue, RES grey, SUS red), ordered by batch within each group; dark circle and line, batch-adjusted group mean and 95% confidence interval (linear model value ~ batch + group, per sex). No main readout differed between groups after correction (two-tier Benjamini-Hochberg). **b**, Batch-adjusted Cohen's d (group difference / residual SD) for the main readouts and for every measure with at least one comparison at adjusted p < 0.05 (black dot); colour limit ±1. The d of all 87 measures are in Supplementary Fig. X. **c**, Locomotion index (mean of the distance travelled in the five test sessions, each z-scored against the controls of the same sex) by group; brackets, p < 0.05. Exploratory: the index was defined after most nominal group-by-sex interactions turned out to be locomotion measures; group-by-sex interaction F(2, 107) = 4.08, P = 0.020. **d**, Cohen's d of the distance travelled in each session and of the index. Females, 12 CON, 28 RES, 18 SUS; males, 12 CON, 30 RES, 17 SUS (open field 4, social preference 1 and novel object D2 1 animal fewer). *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001.

**Extended Data Fig. 11 | Physiology and the spread of the outcome, females and males analysed separately.**
**a**, Main physiology readouts: weight gain from the first to the last weighing, adrenal and spleen weight per 100 g body weight, corticosterone rise (response minus baseline) and sucrose preference over all tests. Four of them are components of the composite outcome (CombZ) and the weight gain shares the weighings of the fifth, so differences between RES and SUS in these measures follow from how the groups were defined. Dots, means and intervals as in Extended Data Fig. 10a; brackets, adjusted p < 0.05 (two-tier Benjamini-Hochberg). Damaged or incomplete organs (22 measurements of 9 animals, dissection notes) are excluded. **b**, Batch-adjusted Cohen's d of all 19 physiology measures; black dot, adjusted p < 0.05. **c**, CombZ of control (CON) and stress-exposed (SIS) animals; SIS animals coloured by outcome group. Lines, control mean and one population SD of the controls above and below; the lower line is the susceptibility cut-off. Top left, SD ratio SIS / CON (batch and condition means removed) with its 95% residual-bootstrap interval and the Brown-Forsythe P. **d**, SD ratio SIS / CON for CombZ, its six components and the main readouts of the conventional tests; black, adjusted P < 0.05 (CombZ, P < 0.05; components and readouts, Benjamini-Hochberg within each set); intervals beyond the axis are drawn to its end.
