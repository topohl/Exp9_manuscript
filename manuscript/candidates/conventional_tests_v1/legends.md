# Draft legends: Extended Data 10, 11 and 12 (candidates)

Candidates only; the numbering follows the free publication identities (`extended_data_10` to `_12`) and may change at submission. Source: SISanalyzer bundle `ctp_v4_20261016_218ed1f` (`source_data/SISanalyzer/`). The panels carry short labels; the full measure names are below and in the per-panel source data.

**Extended Data Fig. 10 | Conventional behaviour tests and locomotion, females and males analysed separately.**
**a**, Main readout of each test: elevated plus maze, share of time in the open arms; open field, share of time in the centre; novel object recognition, novel-object preference D2; social preference, preference D2 for the mouse over the empty cup (phase 1) and for the new over the familiar mouse (phase 2). Novel-object D2 is one of the six CombZ components (Extended Data Fig. 11d; in batch 1 the stored component comes from an earlier automated scoring of the same videos), so a RES–SUS difference in it, and in the other novel-object measures (b), follows partly from how the groups were defined. Dots, individual animals (CON slate blue, RES grey, SUS red), ordered by batch within each group; dark circle and line, batch-adjusted group mean and 95% confidence interval (linear model value ~ batch + group, per sex). No main readout differed between groups after correction (two-tier Benjamini-Hochberg). **b**, Batch-adjusted Cohen's d (group difference / residual SD) of the main readouts and of every measure with at least one comparison at adjusted P < 0.05 (dot); colour limit ±1. SIS, all stress-exposed animals (RES and SUS together). All 87 measures: Supplementary Fig. X. **c**, Locomotion index by group: the distance travelled in each of the five test sessions, z-scored against the controls of the same sex, averaged over the sessions an animal has (at least three). Dots, means and intervals as in a; grey line, z = 0. Exploratory and not corrected: the index was defined after most nominal group-by-sex interactions turned out to be locomotion measures. Females, RES − SUS d = −0.63, P = 0.048 (SUS − CON d = 0.71, P = 0.065); males, RES − CON d = 0.73, P = 0.039, RES − SUS d = 0.66, P = 0.044; group-by-sex interaction F(2, 107) = 4.08, P = 0.020. **d**, Cohen's d of the distance travelled in each session and of the index; colour as in b. Females, 12 CON, 28 RES, 18 SUS; males, 12 CON, 30 RES, 17 SUS. Fewer in the open field (4 females: 2 CON, 2 RES), the social preference test (1 female CON) and novel-object D2 (1 male SUS).

**Extended Data Fig. 11 | Physiology and the spread of the outcome, females and males analysed separately.**
**a**, Main physiology readouts: weight gain from the first to the last weighing; adrenal and spleen weight per 100 g body weight; corticosterone rise (response minus baseline); sucrose preference over all tests. Adrenal, spleen, corticosterone rise and sucrose preference are components of the composite outcome (CombZ). The weight gain shown here (weighing 1 to 9) is not, but it comes from the same series of weighings as the CombZ weight-gain component (weighing 2 to 6, 3 to 6 in batch 6), and its last weighing is the body weight by which the organ weights are divided. Differences of RES and SUS from each other and from CON in these measures therefore follow, wholly or partly, from how the groups were defined (SIS against CON does not). Dots, means and intervals as in Extended Data Fig. 10a; brackets, adjusted P < 0.05 (two-tier Benjamini-Hochberg; *adjusted P < 0.05, **adjusted P < 0.01, ***adjusted P < 0.001). In a and b, damaged or incomplete organs (22 measurements of 9 animals, dissection notes) are excluded and 2 further males have no adrenal weight; CombZ, its components (c, d) and the outcome groups use the stored component scores, which include the excluded organs and, for one of these 2 males, an adrenal score computed from the blank weight as 0 mg (group unchanged). **b**, Batch-adjusted Cohen's d of the main readouts and of every physiology measure with at least one comparison at adjusted P < 0.05 (dot), as in Extended Data Fig. 10b. The added rows (adrenal weight in mg, corticosterone response, sucrose preference in test 1) come from the same data as components and carry the caveat of a. All 19 measures: Supplementary Fig. Y. **c**, CombZ of control (CON) and stress-exposed (SIS) animals; SIS animals coloured by outcome group. Solid line, mean of the controls; dashed lines, one population SD of the controls above and below it (the lower one is the susceptibility cut-off). After removing batch and condition means, the SD of SIS animals was 1.71 times that of controls in females (95% CI 1.31–2.36; Brown-Forsythe P = 0.078) and 1.96 times in males (1.34–3.26; P = 0.017). **d**, SD ratio SIS / CON (log scale) with 95% residual-bootstrap interval for CombZ (top), its six components (middle: novel-object D2, sucrose preference, weight gain from weighing 2 to 6 [3 to 6 in batch 6], corticosterone rise, adrenal and spleen weight per 100 g body weight) and the other four main readouts of the conventional tests (bottom). The Brown-Forsythe P values (Benjamini-Hochberg within each set) and the ratios within cages are in the source data. Group sizes as in Extended Data Fig. 10, except in a and b: adrenal weight, 7 females (2 CON, 4 RES, 1 SUS) and 4 males (1 CON, 2 RES, 1 SUS) fewer; spleen weight, 2 females (1 CON, 1 RES) fewer; corticosterone, 1 male RES fewer; sucrose preference in test 1, 2 females (1 CON, 1 SUS) and 1 male RES fewer. In c and d, 12 CON and 46 (females) or 47 (males) SIS animals; the n of each row of d is in the source data.

**Extended Data Fig. 12 | Automated scoring agrees with manual scoring.**
Automated scores (SLEAP pose estimation, SLEAPanalyzer; the values analysed in Extended Data Fig. 10) against manual scores (BORIS) of the same videos, 20 male animals of batch 1, the only batch whose conventional tests were scored by hand. The batch 1 novel-object component of CombZ comes from an earlier scoring and is not validated here. **a**, Elevated plus maze: time in the open arms, closed arms and centre, and number of nose dips. **b**, Novel object recognition: contact time with the novel and the familiar object, total contact, D2; manual values from the raw BORIS exports. **c**, Social preference: contact time with the mouse and with the empty cup and preference D2 (phase 1); contact time with the new and the familiar mouse and novelty D2 (phase 2). Each dot is one animal; dashed line, identity; r, Pearson correlation. The nose-dip smoothing window and the object-contact detector were chosen on these 20 animals against this manual scoring, so the agreement of nose dips (a) and of b is a calibration fit, not an independent validation; the plus-maze times and the social tests were not tuned. One animal has a novel-object D2 of opposite sign in the two scorings. Open-arm entries are not shown: the manual record counts behaviour bouts in the open arms, not zone entries (their correlation is in the source data). The open field was not scored manually.
